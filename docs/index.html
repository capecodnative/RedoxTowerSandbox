THE FIELD GUIDE

Life follows an energy gradient.

A redox couple contains an oxidized and a reduced form. All tower potentials describe reduction: gaining electrons.

1. Choose a donor and acceptor

The donor’s reduced form loses electrons; the acceptor’s oxidized form gains them. The same couple can play either role, depending on its partner.

2. Follow the fall

Electrons tend to move from a lower reduction potential to a higher one. ΔE′ = E′acceptor − E′donor. A positive ΔE′ means a negative ΔG′ for the reaction as written.

3. Read the energy

ΔG′ = −nFΔE′, with F = 96.485 kJ V⁻¹ mol⁻¹. The app balances electron transfer using the smallest common multiple of both half-reactions. Energy is per mole of the displayed net reaction, not per mole of donor or ATP.

Data & assumptions

Rounded teaching values follow the supplied redox tower (−0.43 to +0.82 V). H₂ and O₂ are rounded to −0.42 and +0.82 V. Nitrate/N₂ uses a whole N₂ molecule (10 electrons). Formal acid-balanced equations retain H⁺ for atom and charge balance; pH is buffered at 7. Species labels are fixed: this model does not calculate carbonate, sulfide or acid–base speciation.

These are illustrative thermodynamic comparisons, not predictions of growth, reaction rates, ATP yield or environmental feasibility. Activities, gas pressures, mineral phases and organism physiology matter. Q′ sliders change the complete half-reaction quotient, not one concentration.

Expanded chart entries

The tetrathionate value reproduces the historical estimate in the image; direct measurements subsequently found +0.198 ± 0.004 V. Cytochrome values are representative and vary with protein environment. Carrier equations describe electron and proton bookkeeping; the conserved molecular backbone is implicit.

Both iron rows are reference-only. At pH 7, mineral and carbonate equilibria must be specified before assigning a meaningful iron half-reaction. The +0.76 V row belongs to pH 2 and must not be combined with buffered pH-7 values. Their displayed Fe³⁺ + e⁻ → Fe²⁺ equations are formal chart shorthand.

Photosystems and light

Enable Photosystems to include P680⁺/P680 (+1.20 V), P680⁺/P680* (−0.60 V), P700⁺/P700 (+0.50 V), and P700⁺/P700* (−1.20 V). These are approximate representative midpoint potentials, not universal biochemical standard potentials. Excited states are offered only as donors, and their activity-quotient controls are disabled. Their calculated free-energy changes describe a single transfer from an already excited donor, excluding photon input and regeneration of the reaction center.

The Light reactions view is a qualitative Z-scheme of oxygenic photosynthesis. Each photosystem switch controls light excitation, not the presence of the complex. It illustrates sustained linear flow from water to NADP⁺. Cyclic electron flow and transient transfers from stored reducing equivalents are outside this view.
